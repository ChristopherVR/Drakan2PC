This follows the [release review](issue-1-release-review.md). The reporter used the public v0.1.2 installer. Private captures, converted content, cards and run manifests stay under `build/`. The [comment draft](issue-1-comment-draft.md) follows the reporter's headings and order.

## Surdana cliff

The original screenshot shows a round dark patch on a sloped rock face, grass, a tree and a rail at the right edge. The log identifies a loaded Surdana save. It contains no player coordinates for the screenshot. The first three logged DXR passes had water and reflections on and direct shadows off. Those receipts precede the screenshot and cannot identify its toggle state.

Using the PAL level's object placements and native terrain triangles, an initial western rail test put Rynn at the foot of a steep face. DX11 completed the normal ray-shadow and observed-baseline sweeps on the same deployed executable. The ground camera at the first pose was too close to the face for a persuasive comparison. Private runs are under `build/harness/issue1-cliff-western-rail`.

The final harness pose uses the directional light and four planet-billboard triangles from the archived pre-fix Surdana DXR scene. Projecting the second billboard along the light onto terrain identifies a visible steep receiver near the western rail. The test places Rynn with the editor's native placement aid, waits for the terrain to settle, and captures the face at three yaw angles. It rejects an incorrect settled position, a missing screenshot or incorrect DXR planet membership. The `legacy-sky` diagnostic restores sky-class admission on the same executable; normal rendering keeps the existing sky exclusion. A comparison reproducing the artifact at this slope can attribute the mechanism without assuming the reporter stood at these coordinates.

On canonical deployment `6bfec79362a133ad83fee77c706bbf327b361571e4f699e1444f28698871fbf7`, the DX11 pair reproduces a rounded, crescent-lit shadow edge on the rock with sky admission restored. The current default removes it at all three yaw angles. Both runs exited cleanly and met the scene checks; the DXR metadata changes from one instance of the planet billboard to zero. In the 322 × 196 face crop, 1,707 pixels brighten by more than three RGB levels and 21 darken by that amount. The difference image isolates the curved patch on the rock; moving vegetation elsewhere was excluded from this measurement. Original captures and the crop are in `build/harness/issue1-cliff-planet-paired`.

The same crop on WebGPU shows the curved edge with the legacy diagnostic and its removal under the default. In that crop 1,367 pixels brighten by more than three RGB levels and 31 darken. Diagonal shading bands elsewhere on the WebGPU slope are present in both cases. DX11 cliff-face camera origins differ by less than one world unit between the two runs. The physical sky-caster fix predates this investigation; this pass provides a controlled visual reproduction on both backends.

The original German PC source, timing of every fade, completeness of every prop, and visual parity with the entire original cinematic are separate checks.

## City pickups

The stock Full Mana pickup at Z 425685 was 14,315 units beyond the old Z minimum of 440000. The match returns out-of-bounds players to a spawn after a grace period. Extending the minimum to 424000 keeps all four original pickup positions inside the match. The new compiled-map regression failed on the old data and passed after the boundary change.

An initial two-peer City run kept all four stock pickup entries and changed the two starting poses to bring the players near pickups 0 and 1. The server accepted player 1's Lastat's Blade claim and player 2's Full Mana claim. Each peer removed the opposite copy, both native inventories held the expected resources, post-inventory movement continued and both scripted exits returned zero. This exploratory run accelerated respawns to eight seconds. Its original checker expected a Blade on both participants and failed on the client's valid Mana. The maintained harness now checks the resource and native type for each pickup, and its authored-site mode uses the stock respawn times. The original failure summary remains in `build/harness/issue1-city-authored-landmarks1`.

Both maintained tests passed on the same final deployment as the shadow comparison. The server accepted pickups 0/1 with their configured 60/30-second respawns, and 2/3 with 20/20 seconds. Both peers removed each other's copies in both matches. RAM shows the Blade in the host inventory in the first match, and native Mana in the other three inventories. Post-inventory movement measured 175/1,300 units for the first pair and 2,445/2,492 for the street pair. All four scripts quit normally, exit 0, without a missing guest branch. The street pair used original City spawn positions 1 and 2 with test headings. The first pair used nearby starting positions. Full routes from all eight normal spawn points and WAN play remain outside these checks. Receipts are in `build/harness/issue1-city-blade-and-mana-final` and `build/harness/issue1-city-street-mana-final`.
