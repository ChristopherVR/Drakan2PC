# Retained OotF cinematic prop display lists

This investigation concerns the imported Intro Owl Face model, which the production pipeline still drops with other rfl17/NPCS generic skinned props. The private retained-prop candidates exercise that path without removing its production exclusion. They are diagnostic assets, not a promoted campaign pack.

## Geometry and native evidence

The original PC mesh renders an intact round owl in a private CPU reference (`build/scratch/ootf-props-anim/owl-source-1.png`). Source model 9 has 68 total vertices; LOD0 uses 66 vertices and 128 triangles, with two joints and two animation clips. Converted Resources model 7351 retains all 128 triangles, the two joints, the source transforms and both clips, with two vertex padding entries. Its 0x0211 display list has one group of 66 vertices and one batch; its longest strip contains 82 elements.

Expanding that converted display list gives exactly the source geometric triangle multiset: zero missing or extra triangles. All 128 triangles consistently reverse the source polygon winding, as intended by the encoder. Corner UVs remain intact, and a CPU render of the decoded list reproduces the original complete owl (`owl-decoded-1.png`). These checks exclude a lost source triangle, a mixed-winding strip expansion, or an unrelated rig/texture substitution as the cause of the observed radial gaps.

A native GIF capture reconstructs the same missing wedges before D3D rendering. Running the same retained pack with `DRAKAN_VU_AOT=0`, `DRAKAN_VU_BATCH=0`, and `PS2X_VU1_THREAD=0` reproduces the gaps and exits cleanly (55 seconds). The issue is therefore not isolated to the AOT specialization. No runtime renderer, VU, model transform, skeleton, animation or global winding change was made.

## Native strip constraints

Auditing 1,593 decoded original models from SYSTEM, RESOURES, NPCS, WORLDCON and LEVEL1ON yields 139,353 strips. Of these, 22,249 are in models with character records and 117,104 are static. Both groups have an empirical maximum of 30 elements. Neither group contains any strip whose vertices and zero terminator cross a 64-element stream boundary. Starts are not required to align to four elements: all four residues occur, and many strips cross 32-element boundaries. Variable runs of zero entries pad the stream before a strip would cross the next 64-element boundary.

This second rule explains why a length-only correction was insufficient. Splitting the Owl's 82-element strip into lengths 30, 30 and 26 placed them at stream offsets 0, 31 and 62. The third strip still crossed the native 64-element boundary. Length-only rendering removed the large wedges but left a thin incorrect diagonal across the beak/cheek.

## Converter correction and checks

The encoder now splits long strips at 30 elements, carrying the final two vertices into the continuation. The advance is 28 triangles, which is even, so resetting strip parity preserves winding. Before serialization it inserts matching zero stream entries and zero UV pairs whenever the next strip and its terminator would cross a 64-element boundary. Batch planning includes this padding when enforcing the existing 448-element capacity; batches after a flush retain the established vertex window rather than reloading unrelated groups.

Synthetic tests cover lengths 30, 31, 32, 58, 59, 86 and 222 with both winding modes. They compare complete oriented triangle/UV multisets, inspect raw block boundaries and zero UV padding, and enforce batch capacity. A separate 85-quad case fits under the old unpadded batch count but requires a padding-aware flush; it verifies 168 plus 2 triangles across two batches and no extra second-batch vertex loads. Full formats validation passes: 329 tests in 30 files, TypeScript compilation, Prettier and diff checks.

The change affects only display lists synthesized by the converter. Original native records are neither rewritten nor re-encoded. The retained-prop exclusion remains unchanged: additional solo/co-op lifecycle and actor-rig acceptance is still needed before restoring those objects in the production campaign. This bounded rendering repair does not establish full cinematic or campaign fidelity.

## Rigid cinematic room: missing draw representation

The source-camera comparison establishes missing scenery rather than an intentional empty backdrop. The original TowerRoom mesh projected from the captured late native camera shows stone walls, floor, windows and the mirror frame. The CPU reference uses the captured camera/room bases, source materials and the documented 2048 model-to-world factor; its approximate focal length is not a pixel-exact native camera reproduction. Native RAM independently confirms the unit factor: all 449 loaded vertices equal source coordinates multiplied by 2048 with zero error. All 722 loaded oriented triangles equal the source triangulation, and the model plus all 13 texture descriptors are ready. `room-native-geometry-audit.json` records the comparison.

A native GIF capture nevertheless contains no corresponding room-sized, 13-material geometry submission: the sampled frame has two 526-triangle character groups and four quad triangles. This places the failure after successful geometry/resource loading. TowerRoom is rigid, with no skeleton; its converted model also lacked the 0x0211 strip draw representation. Most original rigid rfl17 generic-prop models inspected in RESOURES/NPCS carry that representation, including spears, shields, shovels, snowballs and Ballista parts. A few original records lack it, so that audit alone is not proof of universal native behavior.

The decisive private comparison adds a generated 0x0211 to TowerRoom 7813, with the equivalent vertex/polygon layout required by its three vertex windows. It retains 722 triangles, 13 materials, original textures, source placement, camera, lighting and sequence timing. Fifteen batches use the completed strip-length/block-boundary rules. The late native image now shows textured stone walls and the gold mirror frame around falling Delon, in place of the earlier flat quadrilateral surrounded by black. The run exits cleanly after 83 wall seconds with no watchdog or null-controller fault. Evidence is `isolated-direct-delon-room-dl/scene_late.png`; the control is `isolated-direct-delon-end-scene/scene_late.png`, both under `build/scratch/ootf-late-prop-fixtures`.

The production correction requests a display list for models used by source rfl17 generic props. Other ordinary static classes retain their existing conversion path, and original native records remain untouched. When another class imported a shared rigid model first, the builder upgrades the cached model in place, retaining its mapped resource id; repeated requests do not duplicate records or counters. Existing skinned conversion already produces its display list and remains on that path. Required first-import generation failures are reported as failed model imports rather than silently returning a non-drawing model.

Synthetic regressions cover both import orders, repeated-request idempotence, preserved mapped ids/materials/triangles/UVs/vertex positions, one model conversion count, and the ordinary-static no-display-list path. All 340 formats tests in 32 files, TypeScript compilation, Prettier and diff checks pass. This is a bounded isolated ending-presentation correction, not normal boss defeat or uninterrupted campaign completion; coherent full-pack regeneration and broader gameplay acceptance remain separate.

## Bounds and helper-model stability in coherent regeneration

The first regeneration exposed two independent side effects before any earned card was loaded. `withDisplayList` clears model bounds and inserts zero padding; blindly regenerating bounds from that layout changed cached class sphere/box values. Separately, two genuine bounds-only models had zero polygons and failed required display-list construction. The first shifted model was 7340 `Bounding Vertices`; losing the two helpers removed `Current Mover` and `catching fire object` class variants and shifted later resource ids. Both preliminary packs are rejected and remain private (`ootf-prop-dl` and `ootf-prop-dl-bounds`). Neither was used with a progression card.

The narrow rigid-required helper now retains the original effective sphere/box prefix (authored when present, otherwise synthesized from the original geometry), while rebuilding the index-dependent polygon hierarchy from original unpadded vertices and the new polygon count. It does not copy an old polygon hierarchy after reordering. Padding cannot enlarge the physical bounds. Models selected by the pre-existing named display-list rule keep their established path and effective bounds. Genuine zero-polygon geometry/bounds helpers keep their resource ids without a pointless draw list; the earlier rejection of drawable models whose textures drop all polygons remains in force. Skinned conversion is unchanged.

Synthetic regressions cover both import/cache orders with positive-only geometry and padding, authored conservative bounds and absent-source bounds, exactly one reference to each rebuilt polygon, byte-identical legacy named-model behavior, retained helper and subsequent model ids, and missing-texture rejection. All 347 formats tests in 32 files, TypeScript compilation, Prettier and diff checks pass.
